AI hiring, tested for repeatability

The same CV got two different hiring verdicts.

The same fictional CV went through the published tool 5 times with the settings unchanged. Goalpost checks whether the decision, the reasons and the advice stay put.

It also checks its own measuring process. The reader must clear a basic repeatability bar; an instability claim faces an extra margin. If an applicable rule fails, Goalpost withholds the affected measure or claim.

Goalpost has withheld a measure or claim 3 times.

The hiring tools answer in prose. A separate AI, called the reader, turns each reply into comparable lists. Apparent variation could come from the reader rather than the hiring tool.

Apply two reporting rules in order

Basic repeat bar: ≥ 0.90. Below it, withhold the affected measure.

Extra instability margin. Even after the basic bar passes, a claim that something is unstable must clear this stricter rule.

Every applicable rule passed

The measure or claim is eligible to report.

An applicable rule failed

Stop. Withhold the affected measure or claim.

The gate removes reader repeatability as one source of measurement noise. It cannot establish whether the reader is correct; a consistently wrong reader could still pass. “Eligible to report” means that every applicable reader-repeatability rule passed.

Three questions, asked of identical runs

Goalpost measures whether the same input gets the same output. Accuracy and fairness are outside this audit.

Decision

Did the tool keep the same hire, reject or unclear verdict?

Reasons

Did it discuss the same topics? A repeated topic is not automatically a stable explanation: in ⅓ – ½ of paired comparisons, the same topic switched from counting for the candidate to counting against them.

Advice

Did it recommend the same changes? Audit #1’s exact grouped-overlap score was 0.448, on a scale where 1.00 means the compared lists match. The score compares overlap between lists; run percentages use a different measure.

Lock the test material

25 fictional CVs: five for each of five roles, with one job spec per role. No real people. Cases, prompts and code are hash-pinned before the run.

Repeat without tuning

Each CV runs 5 identical times: 25 cases × 5 runs = 125 pipeline runs per audit, at the published tool’s own settings and pinned commit.

Translate prose

The reader creates lists. Synonyms are grouped under a committed, versioned taxonomy; ungrouped values stay in the evidence.

Gate, then report

The reader repeat-check runs before any prose-based measure is published. Every call, hash, version and cost goes into the evidence file and a one-page report.

The three refusals
  1. 27 Jul

    Refusal 1: The stricter margin withheld the favourite claim

    The first reader scored 0.904 on reasons and 0.902 on advice. Both passed the 0.90 bar, but 0.904 was 0.051 short of the 0.955 margin required for the instability claim. A rebuilt v3 reader later passed, but it had studied the target transcripts. That dependence is disclosed.

  2. 08 Aug

    Refusal 2: The target-informed reader failed on the control

    The same v3 reader was less repeatable on prose without the target’s structure. The reported control figures therefore come from the v3 + gemma reader that passed on both sides of the comparison.

  3. 08 Aug

    Refusal 3: The frozen primary reader failed

    For Audit #2, the primary and only backup reader were locked before any target reply existed. The primary failed; the backup then passed at 0.989 / 0.975. Their target measurements agree to about ±0.01. The failed calculation stays in the audit trail; the reported findings come from the backup.

Plain-language glossary and score caveats
Reader
A separate AI that turns a hiring tool’s prose into lists that can be compared.
Self-agreement
The reader repeat-check on the exact same reply. It measures repeatability, not correctness.
Lens
The technical record’s name for a particular reader model and instruction setup.
Cluster-level
Equivalent wording is grouped before comparison. The ungrouped, raw-word result is also retained and is always lower in the results shown here.
Corpus
The locked collection of fictional CV/job cases.
Temperature
A randomness-related setting. Goalpost uses each published tool’s own setting; T = 0 labels the lab setting intended to minimise randomness.
Stability score
An average across comparisons, not the percentage of candidates that flipped. That is why 3 / 25 and 0.968 can both describe Audit #1.
